Emergency calculators

37 emergency calculators and scores. Each one shows its bands and its source, and works offline.

  • Shock index

    Heart rate over systolic pressure.

    Source: Allgöwer M, Schweizer 1967.

  • Parkland burn formula

    24-hour crystalloid after burns.

    Source: Baxter CR, Shires T, 1968.

  • Adult defibrillation

    Energy by shock number.

    Source: Resuscitation Council guidance.

  • Adrenaline infusion

    mL/hour for a µg/kg/min target.

    Source: Standard infusion derivation.

  • Canadian CT head rule

    Who needs a CT after minor head injury.

    Source: Stiell IG et al, Lancet 2001.

  • Ottawa ankle rule

    Who needs an ankle X-ray.

    Source: Stiell IG et al, JAMA 1993.

  • Ottawa knee rule

    Who needs a knee X-ray.

    Source: Stiell IG et al, JAMA 1995.

  • Canadian C-spine rule

    Who needs cervical imaging after trauma.

    Source: Stiell IG et al, JAMA 2001.

  • qSOFA

    Bedside screen for sepsis mortality risk.

    Source: Singer M et al, JAMA 2016.

  • SIRS criteria

    Systemic inflammatory response.

    Source: Bone RC et al, Chest 1992.

  • NEWS2

    Track-and-trigger score for deteriorating adults.

    Source: Royal College of Physicians, NEWS2 2017.

  • Alvarado score

    Likelihood of appendicitis.

    Source: Alvarado A, Ann Emerg Med 1986.

  • SOFA score

    Organ dysfunction across six systems; the sepsis definition anchor.

    Source: Vincent JL et al, Intensive Care Med 1996.

  • NEXUS chest CT criteria

    Blunt chest trauma patients who can safely skip chest CT.

    Source: Rodriguez RM et al, J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2013.

  • Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS)

    Bedside physiology score that flags ward or ED patients at risk of deterioration.

    Source: Subbe CP et al, QJM 2001.

  • Revised Trauma Score (RTS)

    Weighted physiological trauma score from GCS, systolic BP and respiratory rate.

    Source: Champion HR et al, J Trauma 1989.

  • TRISS survival probability

    Probability of survival after trauma from RTS, ISS, age and mechanism.

    Source: Boyd CR et al, J Trauma 1987 (major trauma outcome study); coefficients as implemented in the MTOS TRISS model.

  • MGAP trauma score

    Prehospital/ED trauma mortality score from Mechanism, GCS, Age and arterial Pressure.

    Source: Sartorius D et al, Crit Care Med 2010.

  • GAP trauma score

    Simplified MGAP without mechanism: GCS, Age and systolic Pressure.

    Source: Kondo Y et al, Crit Care 2011.

  • Injury Severity Score (ISS)

    Anatomical trauma severity: sum of squares of the worst AIS in the three most injured body regions.

    Source: Baker SP et al, J Trauma 1974.

  • New Injury Severity Score (NISS)

    Sum of squares of the three most severe injuries, whatever their body region.

    Source: Osler T, Baker SP, Long W, J Trauma 1997.

  • TASH score (Trauma Associated Severe Haemorrhage)

    Predicts the need for massive transfusion after major trauma.

    Source: Yücel N et al, J Trauma 2006.

  • ABC score (massive transfusion)

    Four bedside items that predict massive transfusion in trauma, no labs needed.

    Source: Nunez TC et al, J Trauma 2009.

  • Denver criteria (blunt cerebrovascular injury)

    Expanded Denver signs and risk factors for screening blunt carotid and vertebral injury.

    Source: Biffl WL et al, Ann Surg 2002; expanded by Burlew CC et al, J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2012.

  • Lactate clearance

    Percentage fall in lactate between two samples during resuscitation.

    Source: Nguyen HB et al, Crit Care Med 2004.

  • Base deficit shock classification

    Grades hypovolaemic shock in trauma by admission base deficit rather than vital signs.

    Source: Mutschler M et al, Crit Care 2013.

  • Modified Brooke burn formula

    First-24-hour crystalloid estimate for adult burns at 2 mL/kg/%TBSA.

    Source: Pruitt BA Jr; American Burn Association, Advanced Burn Life Support Provider Manual 2018.

  • Rule of Nines (burn area)

    Adult estimate of burned total body surface area by region.

    Source: Wallace AB, Lancet 1951.

  • Pittsburgh knee rule

    Which acute knee injuries need an X-ray.

    Source: Seaberg DC, Jackson R, Am J Emerg Med 1994.

  • Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS)

    Predicts amputation after severe lower-limb trauma.

    Source: Johansen K et al, J Trauma 1990.

  • GCS-Pupils score (GCS-P)

    GCS minus a pupil reactivity score, for head-injury prognosis.

    Source: Brennan PM, Murray GD, Teasdale GM, J Neurosurg 2018.

  • Rapid Emergency Medicine Score (REMS)

    In-hospital mortality from age and six ED vital signs, for non-surgical patients.

    Source: Olsson T, Terent A, Lind L, J Intern Med 2004.

  • Paracetamol nomogram ratio

    Level divided by the 100 mg/L treatment line for a single acute overdose.

    Source: Rumack BH, Matthew H, Pediatrics 1975; MHRA single treatment line (100 mg/L at 4 h), 2012.

  • Sgarbossa criteria

    Detects acute MI in the presence of left bundle branch block or a paced rhythm.

    Source: Sgarbossa EB et al, N Engl J Med 1996.

  • HINTS exam (plus)

    Bedside eye exam separating central (stroke) from peripheral acute vestibular syndrome.

    Source: Kattah JC et al, Stroke 2009; HINTS plus: Newman-Toker DE et al, Acad Emerg Med 2013.

  • ROSIER scale

    Recognition Of Stroke In the Emergency Room — separates stroke from mimics.

    Source: Nor AM et al, Lancet Neurol 2005.

  • Revised Baux score

    Burn mortality estimate: age plus %TBSA, plus 17 for inhalation injury.

    Source: Osler T, Glance LG, Hosmer DW, J Trauma 2010.
