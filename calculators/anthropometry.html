Anthropometry calculators

25 anthropometry calculators and scores. Each one shows its bands and its source, and works offline.

  • Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST)

    Adult malnutrition risk from BMI, recent weight loss and acute illness.

    Source: BAPEN Malnutrition Advisory Group, The ‘MUST’ Explanatory Booklet 2003.

  • Nutritional Risk Screening 2002 (NRS-2002)

    Hospital nutrition risk from nutritional status, disease severity and age.

    Source: Kondrup J et al, Clin Nutr 2003 (ESPEN).

  • Malnutrition Screening Tool (MST)

    Two-question malnutrition screen: weight loss and poor appetite.

    Source: Ferguson M et al, Nutrition 1999.

  • Mini Nutritional Assessment – Short Form (MNA-SF)

    Six-item nutrition screen for older adults.

    Source: Rubenstein LZ et al, J Gerontol 2001; Kaiser MJ et al, J Nutr Health Aging 2009.

  • Short Nutritional Assessment Questionnaire (SNAQ)

    Quick inpatient screen from weight loss, appetite and nutritional support.

    Source: Kruizenga HM et al, Clin Nutr 2005.

  • Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (GNRI)

    Nutrition-related morbidity risk in older patients from albumin and weight relative to ideal.

    Source: Bouillanne O et al, Am J Clin Nutr 2005.

  • CONUT score (Controlling Nutritional Status)

    Undernutrition grade from albumin, lymphocyte count and cholesterol.

    Source: Ignacio de Ulíbarri J et al, Nutr Hosp 2005.

  • Prognostic Nutritional Index (Onodera)

    Surgical risk from albumin and total lymphocyte count.

    Source: Onodera T, Goseki N, Kosaki G, Nihon Geka Gakkai Zasshi 1984.

  • Nutritional Risk Index (Buzby)

    Malnutrition grade from albumin and current versus usual weight.

    Source: Buzby GP et al, Am J Clin Nutr 1988 (VA TPN Cooperative Study).

  • Basal energy expenditure (Harris-Benedict)

    Original 1919 basal metabolic rate equation.

    Source: Harris JA, Benedict FG, Proc Natl Acad Sci USA 1918; Carnegie Institution 1919.

  • Fat-free mass index (FFMI)

    Fat-free mass corrected for height; a phenotypic criterion for malnutrition.

    Source: Cederholm T et al, Clin Nutr 2015 (ESPEN malnutrition consensus); Schutz Y et al, Int J Obes 2002.

  • Mid-upper arm circumference (adult)

    Estimates BMI category when weight or height cannot be measured.

    Source: BAPEN, The ‘MUST’ Explanatory Booklet 2003.

  • Body fat from skinfolds (Durnin-Womersley)

    Body density from four skinfolds, converted to percent fat with the Siri equation.

    Source: Durnin JVGA, Womersley J, Br J Nutr 1974; Siri WE 1961.

  • Body mass index

    BMI with Asian and standard bands.

    Source: WHO; Asian cut-offs: WHO Expert Consultation, Lancet 2004.

  • Body surface area

    Mosteller BSA for dosing.

    Source: Mosteller RD, N Engl J Med 1987.

  • Ideal body weight

    Devine formula.

    Source: Devine BJ, Drug Intell Clin Pharm 1974.

  • Adjusted body weight

    Weight for lipophilic drugs in obesity.

    Source: Standard dosing convention, 40% correction.

  • Waist-to-height ratio

    Central adiposity screen.

    Source: Ashwell M et al, Nutr Res Rev 2012.

  • Weight loss percentage

    Change from usual weight.

    Source: Standard nutrition assessment.

  • Resting energy expenditure

    Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

    Source: Mifflin MD et al, Am J Clin Nutr 1990.

  • Protein requirement

    1.2 g/kg in illness, 1.5 in burns.

    Source: ESPEN nutrition guidance.

  • BMI prime

    BMI as a fraction of the upper normal limit.

    Source: Gadzik TS, 2006.

  • Lean body mass (Boer)

    Fat-free mass estimate.

    Source: Boer P, Am J Physiol 1984.

  • Body adiposity index

    Adiposity without weight.

    Source: Bergman RN et al, Obesity 2011.

  • Waist to hip ratio

    Central fat distribution.

    Source: WHO waist circumference guidance.
