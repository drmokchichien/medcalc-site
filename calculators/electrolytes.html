Electrolytes calculators

23 electrolytes calculators and scores. Each one shows its bands and its source, and works offline.

  • Corrected sodium (glucose)

    Sodium corrected for hyperglycaemia.

    Source: Hillier TA et al, Am J Med 1999.

  • Serum osmolality

    Calculated osmolality and gap.

    Source: Standard derivation; compare with a measured sample.

  • Anion gap

    Screens for high anion gap acidosis.

    Source: Standard acid-base derivation.

  • Delta ratio

    Splits high anion gap acidosis into mixed pictures.

    Source: Standard acid-base derivation.

  • Corrected calcium

    Adjusts total calcium for albumin.

    Source: Payne RB et al, BMJ 1973.

  • Winter’s formula

    Expected compensation in metabolic acidosis.

    Source: Winter SD et al, 1967.

  • Osmolal gap

    Measured minus calculated osmolality.

    Source: Standard toxicology derivation.

  • Urine anion gap

    Distinguishes renal from extrarenal acidosis.

    Source: Standard acid-base derivation.

  • Anion gap (with potassium)

    Anion gap including potassium.

    Source: Standard derivation.

  • Bicarbonate deficit

    Bicarbonate needed to reach the target.

    Source: Standard acid-base derivation.

  • Sodium correction rate

    Change in sodium per litre of fluid.

    Source: Adrogué HJ, Madias NE, N Engl J Med 2000.

  • Delta gap (delta-delta)

    Rise in anion gap minus fall in bicarbonate: finds a second metabolic disorder.

    Source: Wrenn K, Ann Emerg Med 1990.

  • Albumin-corrected anion gap

    Anion gap adjusted upward for low albumin so a hidden high-gap acidosis is not missed.

    Source: Figge J, Jabor A, Kazda A, Fencl V, Crit Care Med 1998.

  • Effective serum osmolality (tonicity)

    Osmolality from sodium and glucose only — the part that moves water across cells.

    Source: Kitabchi AE et al, Diabetes Care 2009 (ADA hyperglycaemic crises consensus).

  • Standard base excess

    Metabolic component of acid-base status, independent of pCO2 (Van Slyke equation).

    Source: CLSI C46-A2, Blood gas and pH analysis, 2009 (Van Slyke equation).

  • Apparent strong ion difference (Stewart)

    Strong cations minus strong anions; low values mean strong-ion (e.g. hyperchloraemic) acidosis.

    Source: Stewart PA, Can J Physiol Pharmacol 1983; Kellum JA, Crit Care 2000.

  • Strong ion gap (Figge–Kellum)

    Apparent minus effective SID: quantifies unmeasured anions, corrected for albumin and phosphate.

    Source: Kellum JA, Kramer DJ, Pinsky MR, J Crit Care 1995; Figge J et al, J Lab Clin Med 1992.

  • Transtubular potassium gradient (TTKG)

    Estimates potassium secretion in the cortical collecting duct.

    Source: Ethier JH, Kamel KS, Magner PO, Lemann J, Halperin ML, Am J Kidney Dis 1990.

  • Electrolyte-free water clearance

    Volume of water excreted free of sodium and potassium — predicts where serum sodium is heading.

    Source: Rose BD, Am J Med 1986.

  • Respiratory compensation in metabolic alkalosis

    Measured pCO2 against the expected 40 + 0.7 × (HCO3 − 24).

    Source: Berend K, de Vries APJ, Gans ROB, N Engl J Med 2014.

  • Metabolic compensation in respiratory acidosis

    Measured bicarbonate against expected: +1 (acute) or +4 (chronic) mmol/L per 10 mmHg rise in pCO2.

    Source: Berend K, de Vries APJ, Gans ROB, N Engl J Med 2014.

  • Metabolic compensation in respiratory alkalosis

    Measured bicarbonate against expected: −2 (acute) or −4 (chronic) mmol/L per 10 mmHg fall in pCO2.

    Source: Berend K, de Vries APJ, Gans ROB, N Engl J Med 2014.

  • Urine osmolal gap

    Measured minus calculated urine osmolality; about twice the urine ammonium.

    Source: Kim GH, Han JS, Kim YS et al, Am J Kidney Dis 1996.
